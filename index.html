01 — Abstract

Background

Intravenous opioids have a faster onset and greater euphoric potential than oral or subcutaneous routes, with observational studies linking inpatient intravenous exposure to higher odds of subsequent opioid-related harms. An urban academic opioid standard prioritized oral and subcutaneous administration and reduced intravenous exposure by 84% without compromised pain control.

Considering that rural hospitals face unique challenges in opioid stewardship, we implemented this opioid standard in two rural hospitals, hypothesizing that favoring oral and subcutaneous opioids over intravenous would reduce parenteral opioid use without increasing pain scores.

Conclusion

Implementing an opioid standard prioritizing oral and subcutaneous routes in two rural hospitals achieved a 73% reduction in intravenous opioid administration and a 47% reduction in total opioid use without worsening pain control. These findings replicate a prior urban, academic study and demonstrate scalability in rural hospitals, including a critical access hospital.

05 — Methods

Study Design & Intervention

```
🏥

Sites

Academic Medical Center (AMC): Mary Hitchcock Memorial Hospital, Unit 1E — 460-bed rural tertiary center. 30-bed general medical unit.

Critical Access Hospital (CAH): Alice Peck Day Memorial Hospital — 25-bed rural CAH with 50–75% subacute rehabilitation patients.

📋

Intervention (BORING OSCARS)

Oral route preferred for patients capable of oral intake. Subcutaneous route preferred when parenteral opioids were necessary.

Education delivered to clinicians, nurses, and pharmacists. Audit and feedback via Epic "Workbench" EMR reporting. Systematic screening of patients with active IV opioid orders.

📊

Outcomes

Primary: IV opioid doses per patient-day

Secondary: Parenteral and total opioid MME per patient-day; doses per IV order

Balancing: Numeric Pain Score (0–10) over first 5 hospital days

🔬

Participants

All patients on the hospital medicine service who received opioids and spent one or more midnights. 55 hospital medicine providers rotating between both sites.

Pre–post design; 15,749 patient-days total across 848 study days. No randomization.

📅

Timeline

CAH pre: Sep 22, 2020 – Jun 21, 2021 (9 months)

AMC pre: Jan 22 – Jun 21, 2021 (5 months)

Crossover: Jun 22 – Oct 22, 2021

Post (both): Oct 23, 2021 – Oct 22, 2022

📐

Statistics

Descriptive statistics at patient level. Categorical variables: χ² test. Continuous variables: Welch's t-test (normal) or Wilcoxon rank-sum (non-normal).

Shapiro-Wilk test for normality. Two-tailed p-values; significance at p < 0.05.

```
